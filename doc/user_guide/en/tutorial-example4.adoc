FT4 is designed for contesting, particularly on the HF bands and 6
meters.  Compared with FT8 it is 3.5 dB less sensitive and requires
1.6 times the bandwidth, but it offers the potential for twice the QSO
rate.

.Main Window:
- Select *FT4* on the *Mode* menu.
- Double-click on *Erase* to clear both text windows.

.Wide Graph Settings:

- *Bins/Pixel* = 5, *Start* = 100 Hz, *N Avg* = 1
- Adjust the width of the Wide Graph window so that the upper
frequency limit is approximately 3300 Hz.

.Open a Wave File:

- Select *File | Open* and navigate to
+...\save\samples\FT4\200514_182053.wav+.  The waterfall and *Band
Activity* window should look something like the following screen shots.
This sample file was recorded during a practice contest test session, so
most of the decoded messages use the *FT Roundup* message formats.

- Click with the mouse anywhere on the waterfall display. The green Rx
frequency marker will jump to your selected frequency, and the Rx
frequency control on the main window will be updated accordingly.

- Do the same thing with the *Shift* key held down.  Now the red Tx
frequency marker and its associated control on the main window will
follow your frequency selections.

- Do the same thing with the *Ctrl* key held down.  Now both colored 
markers and both spinner controls will follow your selections.

- Now double-click on any of the lines of decoded text in the *Band
Activity* window.  Any line will show similar behavior, setting
Rx frequency to that of the selected message and leaving Tx frequency
unchanged.  To change both Rx and Tx frequencies, hold *Ctrl* down
when double-clicking.

.Best S+P Button

The FT4 user interface includes a button labeled *Best S+P*.

image::Best_S+P.png[align="center"]

Before starting a QSO, click *Best S+P* during an Rx cycle to arm the
program to examine all standard CQ messages in that decoder request,
including messages decoded before the button was clicked. After decoding
finishes successfully, the program selects at most one eligible QSO
partner using the normal automatic-selection path. A new DXCC entity has
first priority; a callsign not worked on the current band has second
priority. Within either category, the first eligible decode wins.
Filters, ignored-call rules, and current logbook history are checked at
completion, allowing another candidate to be selected if an earlier
candidate is no longer eligible.
If the operating band is unknown, the new-entity category remains
available; callsign-on-band eligibility requires a known band.

Enable either or both categories under *Settings | Advanced | Best S+P*.
New profiles enable both. Existing profiles initialize these options
once from the enabled *New DXCC* and *New Call on Band* highlighting
rules. Highlight colors, enabled rules, rule order, UI language, and the
option to show DXCC/worked-before information do not affect selection.
Consequently, stations previously masked by a higher-priority color rule
can become eligible. Both categories must not be disabled when arming.

Worked-before matching follows *Highlight by Mode* and *Include extra WAE
entities* on *Settings | Colors*. These options determine whether history
is matched per mode and whether the entity definition includes the extra
WAE entities.

Best S+P cannot be armed while *Wait & Call* or *Wait & Pounce* is actively
choosing from CQ messages. Turn that feature off first. Activating either
feature while Best S+P is armed cancels Best S+P with an explanation.
Directed replies, including *Wait & Reply* and replies to your own CQ,
continue to be processed normally. Merely having a callsign in *DX Call*
does not prevent arming. If no CQ qualifies, Best S+P remains armed for
subsequent receptions until selection, cancellation, or timeout.

Click the button again or press *Alt+B* to cancel. Manual station
selection, Halt Tx, an operating-context change, or a request to transmit
also cancels arming. Adjusting the Rx or Tx audio frequency preserves
arming, including waterfall retuning and *Shift+F11/F12* Tx nudges.
Arming expires after two minutes. Status messages
and the button's checked state indicate arming, cancellation, timeout,
and the selected station with its reason. A failed decode cannot select
a station; arming can remain active for the next reception. The button's
accessible description retains the result until the next Best S+P action.
Re-decoding
the same reception does not repeat a completed selection intent;
deliberately rearming creates a new intent. Disk-file requests are
independent, and UDP replay does not participate.

TIP: Keyboard shortcuts *Shift+F11* and *Shift+F12* provide an easy
way to move your FT4 Tx frequency down or up in 90 Hz steps.

TIP: For easy keyboard control of transmitted messages, check
*Alternate F1–F6 bindings* on the *File | Settings | General* tab. In
contest-style operation you can then hit *F1* to solicit a QSO by
sending CQ.  Similarly, keys *F2* to *F5* will send the messages in
entry fields *Tx2* to *Tx5*.  More details on contest-style operation
can be found in {ft4_protocol}.
